*Screech*

*To the biggest Eagles fan I ever met, my father*

Is that time of the year and, for me, is my first time going into the woods of the Island with companion. Going throughout it alone was dreadful, but today I can not feel more concerned. We are all aware that The Wild is cold and feelingless, but sometimes I find it quite cruel and ruthless. A race for your life is exciting when you're young and strong, but actual horror comes from the life of the ones you care about.

The praire is nothing like that maze of torment that lies inside the woods. We need to go far deep inside the nooks drawn by roots and branches in order to find a worth meal, but The Wild, sometimes cruel and ruthless, resulted to put our greatest feast inside a trap where we are the dessert of others. The Wild, so wise and clever but also got a dark sense of humour.

That screech. That hideous noise is the call of our nightmares. Once you listen to it, you better start lashing the ground towards the praire no matter how, no matter what, no matter who is left behind. Those devilish abominations let their massive bodies drop from the highest branches, covering us with their endless shades. Only a mat of scarlett stained feathers embellishes the place where The Wild unleashed its show.

I can barely eat. The branches and leaves fall from my beak as I rather have a look out while my offspring and my mate have theirs. Lack of nourishment is lack of strenght to flee of the screech. It is not a deal if the screech deafens the woods or not, but when will it do is the actual motif. The screech is a fact, not a likelihood. Then, I better unfold my senses after it.

They know the mark. Even the slightest sound that resembles the screech means the beginning of the maneuver. Once I start moving towards the shore as fast as my feet let me, I expect them to follow me. There is no time to explain. They know the safe spot and I hope we all meet there soon. All of my feathers tremble when I notice a worst thing than one of those screeches. A second screech. It seems that I'm not the only one who is teaching their child how to reach food and survive The Wild today. 

As immediate as I can, I look back when I reach the safe spot. Following my steps, one of my children arrives to the place. After that, the woods turn silent and we start the step of the journey back to the praire. My son is not wondering why we are not waiting for its mother or its sibling. Today, two children learnt how to get their meal and survive The Wild. However, mine is about to endure a harder lesson of life from now onwards.
